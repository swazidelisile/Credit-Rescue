Two ways to restructure your debt

Debt mediation

An informal option to manage unsecured debt more affordably while protecting your creditworthiness.

  • Informal restructuring of eligible unsecured debt
  • Monthly instalments potentially reduced by up to 50%
  • Payments distributed through a group of attorneys
  • Home loans and vehicle finance are not included
  • You may stop once your budget is satisfied

Eligibility and outcomes depend on your circumstances and creditor agreements.

Ask about debt mediation

Debt review

A formal restructuring process under the National Credit Act for consumers who are, or are at risk of becoming, over-indebted.

  • One structured repayment plan for included accounts
  • Confirmed by a court or the National Consumer Tribunal
  • Protection for eligible assets such as a home, vehicle and furniture
  • No limit on the amount of debt considered
Your credit profile will show that you are under debt review, you generally cannot take new credit, all eligible accounts must be included, and the repayment period may be longer.

Common questions

What does the free assessment involve?

A consultant reviews your income, expenses and debts, then explains which options, if any, suit you. There is no obligation to proceed.

What is the difference between mediation and debt review?

Mediation is informal and covers unsecured debt. Debt review is formal under the National Credit Act and is confirmed by a court or the Tribunal.

Will this affect my credit record?

Debt review is shown on your credit profile and you generally cannot take new credit while it lasts. Ask us how mediation differs.

Can I include my home loan or car finance?

Not in mediation. Debt review may include them, subject to the process and legal requirements.
